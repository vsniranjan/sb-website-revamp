IEEE MACE SB


History
        The IEEE Student Branch at Mar Athanasius College of Engineering (IEEE MACE SB) has been actively serving the student community for the past 38 years, consistently aligning its initiatives with the vision and mission of IEEE—advancing technology for the benefit of humanity.Driven by a team of passionate engineering students committed to learning and excellence, the Student Branch strives to ensure that every initiative not only helps members develop their technical and professional skills but also empowers them to contribute meaningfully to the ever-evolving world of technology. From hackathons and technical workshops to community outreach and social initiatives, every event is thoughtfully designed to create opportunities for learning, innovation, and collaboration. Through these efforts, IEEE MACE SB continues to support the holistic growth of its members while fostering a strong and vibrant engineering community.

SPARC
   In today's fast-paced world, every decision an individual makes can have a lasting impact on their future. With countless opportunities and career paths available, making informed choices can often be challenging, especially for students beginning their college journey.To address this, IEEE MACE SB has been conducting SPARC for the past five years. Designed primarily for first-year students, SPARC provides a platform where accomplished speakers from diverse fields share their experiences, insights, and guidance. Through their expertise and inspiring journeys, participants gain valuable perspectives on personal growth, career planning, and the opportunities that lie ahead, enabling them to make well-informed decisions and build a strong foundation for their future.


STEM initiatives
Recognizing the importance of nurturing curiosity and innovation from an early age, IEEE MACE SB has taken the initiative to promote STEM (Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics) education among school students. The program aims to spark an interest in STEM disciplines, expose students to emerging technologies, and encourage them to explore careers in engineering, research, and innovation.Through interactive workshops, hands-on activities, demonstrations, and engaging learning sessions, students are introduced to practical applications of scientific and engineering concepts beyond the classroom. These sessions are designed to cultivate critical thinking, problem-solving skills, creativity, and teamwork while making learning an enjoyable experience.Each year, the initiative reaches nearly 500 students across five schools, ensuring broad participation and inclusivity. By providing early exposure to STEM and creating opportunities for experiential learning, IEEE MACE SB strives to inspire the next generation of innovators, equipping them with the confidence and curiosity to pursue their interests and contribute meaningfully to the technological advancements of the future.
The main domains that are taught to students are:
1.IOT and Embedded systems
2.Mechatronics
3.Software development
4.Cybersecurity and Data analytics
All these help students to gain practical knowledge at a young level. We are also happy that many students as part of our STEM initiative in the previous editions  have joined our team this year.






Societal Impact initiatives
IEEE MACE SB has always been committed to organizing initiatives that serve a greater social cause and improve the lives of people. Over the years, the Student Branch has undertaken projects benefiting the elderly, persons with disabilities, and underserved communities. Its initiatives have also focused on addressing real-world challenges, including improving access to clean water and enabling communication in remote tribal villages. Through such impactful projects, IEEE MACE SB continues to uphold IEEE's vision of advancing technology for the benefit of humanity while creating a meaningful difference in society. Some of the initiatives are mentioned below:
i) Light the Lives (LTL)
   Lack of access to reliable energy resources was a major challenge in the Kuttampuzha Grama Panchayat, making it difficult for many residents to meet even their basic daily needs. Recognizing this issue, IEEE MACE SB proposed a sustainable solution to the local authorities. With their support and approval, a team of volunteers stayed in the locality for a week to install two solar power systems, providing the community with a dependable and sustainable source of electricity while improving their quality of life.




ii) Connect the lives (CTL)
   Following the successful completion of Light The Lives (LTL), a nearby tribal colony in Uriyampetty approached IEEE MACE SB with another pressing concern. The children in the village had limited access to quality education, as teachers were often unable to reach the remote locality, and the students lacked reliable transportation to schools in nearby towns.After conducting a detailed survey, IEEE MACE SB identified a sustainable solution. With the support of a teacher residing in the colony, the team established a communication module at the local school, enabling students to access online educational classes and digital learning resources. The local teacher was trained to operate and maintain the facility, ensuring its effective use. A year after its implementation, the project continues to benefit the community by improving access to education and creating new learning opportunities for the children.
iii) Wild Elephant Tracker
Human–wildlife conflict has emerged as a significant challenge in Kerala over the past few years, with increasing incidents affecting both lives and livelihoods. One of the major challenges in mitigating these conflicts is the lack of an effective system to detect wildlife movement and provide timely alerts to authorities, residents, and travellers.To address this issue, IEEE MACE SB, under the leadership of Madhav K. Anil, developed an early warning system capable of detecting elephant movement in forest fringe areas and issuing real-time alerts to nearby communities. By enabling timely communication and rapid response, the project helps reduce the risk of human–elephant encounters, enhancing public safety while promoting coexistence between humans and wildlife.


HACK-A-DDIT
To raise awareness about drug abuse and its harmful effects, IEEE MACE SB, in collaboration with the Lions Club, organized a hackathon that encouraged students to develop innovative solutions to address this growing social issue. The event provided a platform for participants to propose technically feasible and socially relevant ideas aimed at prevention, awareness, and rehabilitation. Beyond fostering innovation, the initiative also helped students gain a deeper understanding of the consequences of substance abuse, promoting informed choices and encouraging a healthy, drug-free lifestyle. 
Hack-Her
Despite significant progress, women continue to be underrepresented in engineering and technology, with global statistics reflecting lower participation compared to their male counterparts. To encourage greater inclusion and empower aspiring women engineers, IEEE MACE SB organizes Hack-her, a 24-hour ideation and implementation challenge exclusively for women. The event provides a supportive platform for participants to innovate, collaborate, and transform their ideas into practical solutions. Beyond fostering technical skills, Hacher promotes confidence, leadership, networking, and peer learning, inspiring more women to pursue and excel in engineering while developing solutions that positively impact the community.
Enhance their lives(ETL)
Menstrual hygiene is an essential aspect of women's health, yet the lack of proper disposal facilities for sanitary pads remains a significant challenge, particularly in schools and rural communities. To address this issue, IEEE MACE SB is planning to establish sanitary pad incinerators and appropriate waste disposal systems in remote schools and underserved localities. This initiative aims to provide a safe, hygienic, and environmentally responsible method for disposing of used sanitary products while promoting menstrual hygiene awareness and improving sanitation facilities for students and the community.
